# Why the FAA Aviation Research Grant (NOFO 20-01) is dead for us

Status: Alef can't be the prime applicant. Parked by the CEO.

## The problem
NOFO 20-01 only takes colleges, universities, and other non-profit 501(c)(3) research institutions, plus non-profit professional associations, hospitals, labs, and museums. It excludes individuals and for-profit companies as prime recipients. There's one narrow opening, for aviation-security research, and that isn't us.

Alef is a for-profit company, so it can't hold the award directly. I checked this before drafting anything, which saved us writing a proposal that couldn't be submitted.

Sources:
- [FAA NOFO 20-01 eligibility summary](https://grantexec.com/grants/ab1321d1-0b50-4ac9-aea9-7e70073603d0)
- [FAA Tech Center, how to apply](http://www.tc.faa.gov/contracts/grants/how.html)
- [FAA Aviation Research Grants main page](https://www.faa.gov/about/office_org/headquarters_offices/ang/grants/research)

## The options I gave the CEO
Drop it and put the time into NSF and NASA, where Alef qualifies outright. Or run it through a university: an FAA ASSURE Center-of-Excellence member, or any university, holds the grant as prime and Alef rides along as an industry subrecipient bringing the test vehicle, the data, and in-kind work. That path works but it means finding and signing up a university partner, which is a separate effort on a longer clock. The security angle isn't worth chasing.

## What happened
The CEO parked it. Focus is NSF and NASA. If a university partner ever materializes, the subrecipient route is there to revisit.
