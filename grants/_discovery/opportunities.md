# Alef Aeronautics grant discovery, top 20

Put together 18 June 2026. Every row has a source. Where a number comes from a secondary source or I couldn't pin it down, the row says VERIFY. I didn't make anything up; when I don't know, the row says so. The time-to-award column is my estimate of how long from submission to money, based on how each program normally runs, so check it against the real solicitation before you count on it.

What the CEO confirmed on 18 June: majority US-individual ownership, which passes the SBIR ownership test; under 500 employees (assumed); SAM.gov active with a UEI already issued; and the company is in R&D, not production. Still unknown: whether there's a Login.gov account.

One thing colors all of this. Federal SBIR/STTR went dark from October 2025 to April 2026 when its authorization lapsed. Congress renewed it through FY2031 on 13 April 2026 (S.3971). Agencies are still clearing the backlog, so treat every date here as soft. Source: [Eva Garland Consulting](https://www.evagarland.com/sbir-reauthorization-where-we-stand/).

Fit key: 🟢 high, 🟡 medium, 🔴 low or indirect, ⚫ military (deprioritized).

## What I'd actually do
NSF is the one to start today. It's open, it doesn't care that we're not military, it needs no government customer, and $305K is real money for a feasibility study. NASA fits the technology better than anything else here, but its PY2026 window already closed, so we write the narrative now and wait for PY2027. Once Alef starts buying manufacturing or lab equipment, the CAEATFA sales-tax exclusion is the best California move, because it wipes out 100% of the sales tax on qualifying gear and explicitly covers advanced-transportation work.

Two rows are on the list only so you know I looked: NSF PFI wants a prior NSF award we don't have, and NSF Engines wants a whole consortium rather than a single startup. And again, the reauthorization backlog can shift any of these dates.
